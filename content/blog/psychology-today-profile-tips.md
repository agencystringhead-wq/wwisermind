For years, a Psychology Today profile was the easiest way for a therapist to get found. For many, it still is. But over the past couple of years, therapists in forums and consultation groups have described their inquiries slowing down, sometimes sharply, and they're understandably asking what changed and what they can do about it.

This guide covers what we actually know about how the directory works, what's just guesswork, how to write a profile that turns views into calls, and how to make sure one directory isn't your only source of clients.

## Is it you, or is it the directory?

Probably a bit of both, and mostly neither.

[ClearHealthCosts](https://clearhealthcosts.com/blog/2026/01/therapist-forums-buzzing-over-drop-in-psychology-today-referrals/) collected reports from therapists whose directory referrals had dropped a lot. Psychology Today, for its part, told them that overall directory visits and total contacts haven't declined, and that it periodically adjusts its algorithm to spread traffic more evenly.

Both can be true. If the directory gets the same number of visitors but has more therapists listed, including large platforms and group practices with many profiles, each individual profile gets a smaller share. Meanwhile, more clients now start their search on Google or in AI tools like ChatGPT, and only check directories later, if at all.

So a drop in calls usually isn't a sign that your profile is "broken." It's a sign that the market around it has changed.

## What we know (and don't) about how Psychology Today ranks profiles

Psychology Today doesn't publish exactly how it orders search results. Its directory terms mention broad factors such as profile completeness, but not how much each one counts. That's important, because a lot of advice online presents guesses as facts.

What's reasonable to say:

- **Completeness likely matters.** Fill in every relevant field: specialties, issues, treatment approaches, insurance, fees, languages, ages, and availability.
- **Order of results changes.** Many therapists notice their position moves without any change on their end. Treat ranking as something you can influence a little, not control.
- **Filters matter more than position.** Clients often filter by insurance, specialty, issue, gender, language or telehealth. If you haven't selected the options that apply to you, you won't show up in those filtered searches at all.

What to be skeptical of: claims that small tricks, like changing your zip code, editing your profile every day, or stuffing in keywords, will reliably push you up. There's no good public evidence for these, and some could make your profile less accurate.

## How to write a Psychology Today profile that gets calls

Being seen is only half the job. Once someone clicks your profile, you have a few seconds to help them feel, "This person gets it." Most profiles lose people here.

### Start with them, not you

The opening lines of your profile are the most read. Many profiles begin with credentials or a general statement about the therapy relationship. Instead, describe what your ideal client is going through, in their words.

> Instead of: "I am a licensed clinical social worker with 12 years of experience using an integrative approach."
>
> Try: "You're good at holding everything together. At work, at home, for everyone else. But lately, the worry doesn't switch off, even at night."

### Then say how you help

After someone feels understood, explain what working with you is like. Keep it concrete. What happens in sessions? What approaches do you use, explained simply? What tends to change for people?

### Be selective with specialties

Choose the issues you genuinely specialize in and can back up with training and experience. A profile listing 40 issues reads as "I do everything," which often feels less trustworthy than a focused one. Professional ethics codes also expect you to represent your competence accurately.

### Use a warm, current photo

Your photo is often the first thing people judge. A recent, well-lit photo where you look approachable does more than a formal headshot from ten years ago.

### Add a short video if you're comfortable

Psychology Today profiles support a video statement. There's no public data showing it improves ranking, but a short, warm video can help a nervous person decide you're safe to call. Keep it under a minute, and make sure nothing confidential is visible in the background.

### Be clear about fees and availability

List your fee, whether you offer a sliding scale, and your insurance or out-of-network details. Keep your "accepting new clients" status current. If you're full, say so, or you'll frustrate people and waste your own time.

### Ask colleagues, never clients, for endorsements

Where endorsements from other professionals are available, they're a good way to show you're respected by peers. Never ask clients for reviews or testimonials; most ethics codes restrict it.

### End with an easy next step

Close your profile with a simple, low-pressure invitation: "I offer a free 15-minute phone consultation. Reach out, and we'll see if we're a good fit."

## Connect your profile to your website

Your Psychology Today profile and your website should work together.

- **Link to your website** from your profile, ideally to a specific specialty or booking page.
- **Use the same name, address and phone number** on both, and on Google. Consistent details help search engines and AI tools trust your information.
- **Make sure your website answers what the profile can't.** People who click through want to see more about you, your approach and your fees.

## Is Psychology Today still worth it?

At $29.95 a month in the US, it only needs to bring in one client a year to pay for itself, so for many therapists it's still worth keeping. The real risk isn't the cost. It's relying on it as your only source of new clients.

The simplest way to decide: track it. Ask every new client how they found you, and add "Psychology Today" as an option on your intake form. Check your profile's view and contact reports each month. If it's sending you clients, keep it. If it hasn't sent anyone in six months, put that time and money into channels you control.

## Don't let one directory be your only front door

Even a great profile depends on someone else's platform and algorithm. The practices with the steadiest caseloads usually have several sources working together:

- **Your own website**, with a page for each specialty and clear fees.
- **Your Google Business Profile**, so you show up in map results for "therapist near me."
- **AI search visibility**, so tools like ChatGPT can find and describe you accurately. We explain how in our post on [how therapy platforms are buying up search](/blog/therapy-platforms-search-private-practice/).
- **Referral relationships** with doctors, school counselors and other therapists.
- **One or two other directories** that fit your niche, such as TherapyDen or your professional association's directory.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why am I not getting referrals from Psychology Today anymore?

Common reasons include more profiles competing in your area, larger platforms listing many clinicians, and more clients starting their search on Google or AI tools. Check that your profile is complete, your filters (insurance, specialties, telehealth) are accurate, and your opening lines speak directly to your ideal client.

### How do I get my Psychology Today profile to show up higher?

Psychology Today doesn't publish its ranking rules, but completeness is a stated factor. Fill in every relevant field, choose specialties you can back up, and keep availability current. Be wary of anyone promising a guaranteed boost.

### Does adding a video help my Psychology Today profile?

There's no public evidence that video improves ranking, but it can make it easier for someone to decide to contact you. Keep it short and warm, and check that nothing confidential is visible.

### How much does Psychology Today cost for therapists?

In the US, the join page lists $29.95 per month with no contract, as of September 2026. Prices can change, so check the current rate before signing up.

### What should I write in my Psychology Today profile?

Open with what your ideal client is going through, in their words. Then explain how you help and what sessions are like, list your fees and insurance details, and end with an easy next step, such as a free consultation.
